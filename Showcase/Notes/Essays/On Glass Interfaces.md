> [!quote] Premise
> An interface can be present without being in the way.

## 1. Why glass

Glass lets an interface show depth without heavy borders. Each layer reveals a little of what lies beneath, so you always know where you are.

Glass lets an interface show depth without heavy borders. Each layer reveals a little of what lies beneath, so you always know where you are. The goal is clarity, not decoration.

## 2. Layers

Panels sit on the canvas, menus sit on panels, and popups sit on menus. Each step up adds blur and a touch more brightness.

Panels sit on the canvas, menus sit on panels, and popups sit on menus. Each step up adds blur and a touch more brightness. The goal is clarity, not decoration.

## 3. Blur

Blur is the main ingredient. Too little and text behind competes with text in front; too much and the glass turns into frosted plastic.

Blur is the main ingredient. Too little and text behind competes with text in front; too much and the glass turns into frosted plastic. The goal is clarity, not decoration.

## 4. Tint

A small tint ties the interface to your accent color. Keep it subtle so content stays the hero.

A small tint ties the interface to your accent color. Keep it subtle so content stays the hero. The goal is clarity, not decoration.

## 5. Refraction

At the edges of real glass, the background bends. A thin band of distortion makes a flat blur feel like an object.

At the edges of real glass, the background bends. A thin band of distortion makes a flat blur feel like an object. The goal is clarity, not decoration.

## 6. Motion

When a button opens a menu, the menu should grow from the button. Shared origins make the interface feel connected.

## 7. Contrast

Text on glass needs care. Test both light and dark backgrounds, and prefer a slightly heavier weight for small labels.

## 8. Restraint

Not everything should be glass. The editor stays calm and readable; glass is for the chrome around it.
